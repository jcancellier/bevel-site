Privacy policy

Last updated: November 22, 2026

This policy covers the iPhone game Bevel: Ricochet Puzzle ("Bevel"), made by Joshua Cancellier, an individual developer ("I", "me"). Questions: joshua.cancellier@icloud.com.

The short version

  • There are no accounts and no sign-up. I never ask for your name, email or phone number.
  • Bevel asks for your birth year once. It stays on your phone and is used only to keep ads suitable for your age: only an age band (under 16, or 16 and over) would ever reach the ad network, never the year.
  • Your progress and settings are saved on your phone.
  • Bevel does not ask to track you across other apps, so it never gets your advertising identifier (IDFA).
  • Bevel keeps a log of anonymous gameplay events on your phone, tied only to a random install ID. Today nothing is sent anywhere.
  • The free version shows ads. An ad network serves them and gets basic device information to do so.
  • Payments go through Apple. I never see your card or your Apple Account.
  • Bevel never reads your location, contacts or photos.

Saved on your phone

Bevel saves your progress (rooms cleared, stars, daily rooms, coins, the looks you own), your settings, the birth year you picked (or that you chose not to say), and a random install ID on your phone. This stays on the device. Apple's own iPhone backups may include it, under Apple's terms.

Gameplay analytics

Bevel keeps a log of gameplay events on your phone so I can later make the game better: for example, where players get stuck, how long a room takes, and whether a hint helped. Each event carries only:

  • a random install ID (16 random characters made on your phone the first time you open Bevel; it is not your device ID or your advertising ID and isn't linked to who you are),
  • the game and its build number,
  • the time of the event,
  • the event itself.

The events are about play, never about you:

EventsWhat they hold
Sessions and roomsA session started; a room started or ended (win, fail or quit), the time it took, shots fired, attempts, glass left, the control scheme, whether a hint or the spare shot was used.

The current builds keep this log on your phone only. Once analytics is switched on, the events would be sent to a privacy-focused analytics service, which would store them for me. That service isn't chosen yet; this page will name it before anything is sent. Like any server, it sees your IP address when the events arrive; I don't use it to identify you. I don't sell these events or use them for advertising.

Ads

The free version of Bevel shows a short ad after some wins, and offers optional videos (for example, one more shot) that you can choose to watch. In the current test builds these ads are simulated: no ad network is in the app yet. The released game will use AppLovin MAX, which picks ads from AppLovin and the ad networks it works with.

To show an ad, the ad network receives information from your phone such as your IP address, device model, iOS version, language, and how you interacted with the ad. This is collected by the ad network under its own privacy policy, not by me.

When ads go live, Bevel will set them up like this:

  • Non-personalized ads. Bevel does not ask for tracking permission, so the ad network can't use your advertising identifier, and Bevel will tell it the player has not agreed to personalized ads or to the sale of their data. If that ever changes, this page will say so first, and Bevel would only ever ask players who are 16 or over.
  • Age. Players under 16, and anyone who chose not to say, are always treated as unable to agree to tracking. Only that age band would reach the ad network, never the year.
  • Ad rating. Bevel's ad settings will ask the networks for ads rated for all ages (G).
  • Remove ads. A one-time purchase turns off all ads.

Purchases

Bevel offers optional one-time purchases (Remove ads, a starter pack of coins and a ball look, Bevel Plus, which is the two together, and packs of looks) through Apple's App Store. Every purchase goes through Apple's own sheet, which asks for Face ID or your Apple Account password. Apple handles the payment and your Apple Account; Bevel only learns whether a purchase was made, so it can unlock it and restore it on your other devices.

Photos and notifications

  • Save clip to Photos. Only when you tap it, Bevel adds a video of your winning shot to Photos. It asks for "add only" access: it can't see or read your photo library.
  • Reminders. If you turn them on, daily reminders are scheduled on your phone. They are not sent from a server.

Children

Bevel is suitable for all ages, but it is made for a general audience and is not in the App Store's Kids category. I don't knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. For parents, Settings has Family setup: it hides every "watch an ad" button and the share, save-clip and challenge buttons, and puts every purchase behind a grown-up's 2-second hold; turning it off asks a grown-up's question. If the birth year picked is under 13, Bevel offers it once; it never turns on by itself. Ads after wins still show, rated for all ages, until Remove ads is bought. If you think a child has sent me personal information, email me and I'll delete it.

Deleting your data

  • Reset progress (Settings) clears your rooms, coins and looks earned on this phone. Purchases made with real money stay, and so does the install ID.
  • Deleting the app removes everything Bevel saved on your phone, including the install ID. A new install gets a new, unconnected ID.
  • To ask about or delete analytics events, email joshua.cancellier@icloud.com.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example in California, the EU or the UK), you may have the right to ask what data about you is held, and to have it corrected or deleted. Because Bevel has no accounts, I usually can't link data to a person; email me and I'll help as far as I can.

Changes

If this policy changes, I'll update this page and the date at the top.
